    How the bidding works

    One processor quoting you is a sales pitch. Five quoting at once is an auction.

    Processors sharpen their pencils when they know they're being compared. That's the whole idea behind Revenue Fuel.

    1. Your statement goes in, your identity stays out

      We hide your merchant ID, bank account and owner details before anything leaves our hands. Processors see your volume, ticket size and card mix, which is all they need to price you.

    2. Every processor gets the same package at the same time

      Each one knows the others are bidding. Nobody gets to be first, and nobody gets to be the only option.

    3. Every bid comes back in the same format

      Interchange-plus pricing, monthly fees, equipment and contract terms, all on one page, so a low rate can't hide a high monthly fee.

    4. You pick the winner, or nobody

      Switch to the lowest bid, use it to get your current processor to match, or stay put. Only the processor you choose ever gets your contact details.

    If no one beats your current pricing, we tell you so in writing.

    You keep your processor, you keep the report, and you've lost nothing but a minute. Most owners find out they're overpaying. Some find out they're fine. Both are worth knowing.

      Fee decoder

      The lines on your statement, translated.

      Grab last month's statement and look for these. If you find two or more, you're very likely overpaying, and the bidding will show by how much.

      Mid-qual / non-qual surchargeMarkup

      Tiered pricing sorts your cards into "qualified," "mid" and "non" buckets, and the processor decides where each card lands. Rewards and business cards almost always end up in the expensive buckets. Interchange-plus pricing removes the buckets entirely.

      PCI non-compliance feeAvoidable

      A monthly penalty, often $20 to $100, for not having a current PCI security questionnaire on file. Filling it out usually stops the charge, and many processors help you do it.

      Regulatory / compliance feeAvoidable

      It sounds official, but no regulator or card network charges it. The processor created it, and you can ask to have it removed.

      Statement feeAvoidable

      A charge for sending you the bill. Many processors don't have one.

      Annual or membership feeNegotiable

      Billed once a year, so it's easy to miss. Plenty of processors charge nothing, and it's one of the easiest fees to get waived.

      Monthly minimumNegotiable

      If your processing fees fall below a set amount, you pay the difference. It hurts seasonal businesses most.

      Network access / assessment feePass-through

      These are real Visa, Mastercard, Discover and Amex fees. They should match the networks' published amounts. Some processors pad them, so we check.

      Equipment leaseReview

      Third-party terminal leases often run 48 months and can't be cancelled. Total payments can be several times the price of buying the same terminal outright.

      Early termination / liquidated damagesReview

      A flat exit fee is manageable. "Liquidated damages" can mean the processor's projected profit for the rest of your term. We read this clause before you decide anything.

      Batch / settlement feeNegotiable

      A small charge each time you close out the day. It adds up to $5 to $10 a month at a daily close, and some processors don't charge it.

      Questions

      What business owners ask us first

      Why would processors bid lower just because others are bidding?

      A processor's markup over interchange is pure negotiation. When a rep thinks you have no other offer, you get the rate card. When the same rep knows four competitors are pricing the same statement at the same time, you get their best number, because the only other outcome is losing the account.

      Is this really free?

      Yes. The report, the bidding and the written quotes cost you nothing. If you switch, the processor you choose pays Revenue Fuel a referral fee. We show that fee on every bid, and we rank bids by what you'd pay, never by what we'd earn.

      Do I have to switch processors?

      No. Many owners use the winning bid to get their current processor to match it, and it often works. If your pricing is already good, your report will say so.

      What happens to my statement?

      The instant report runs in your browser, so the file doesn't leave your device for that step. If you start the bidding, we hide your merchant ID, bank account and owner details before any processor sees it, and we delete the file after 30 days unless you ask us to keep it for Rate Watch.

      I'm in a contract. Is switching worth it?

      Tell us the exit fee and we'll show your savings after paying it. Some processors will cover part of a termination fee to win the bid. We also check for auto-renewal dates and "liquidated damages" clauses that can cost more than a flat fee.

      Will my card terminal still work?

      Often it can be reprogrammed. If it's locked to your processor, or leased, we'll show you the cost of each option, including buying a terminal outright.

      Should I add a surcharge or cash-discount program?

      It can lower your costs, but the rules are strict. Surcharges are banned in some states, capped at 3% by Visa, and never allowed on debit cards. Some customers also push back. We'll tell you what's allowed in your state and what it would really net you before you decide.

      What do I need to upload?

      One recent monthly statement, all pages. Most processors let you download it as a PDF from their online portal. Three months gives processors a better picture and usually a sharper bid.
